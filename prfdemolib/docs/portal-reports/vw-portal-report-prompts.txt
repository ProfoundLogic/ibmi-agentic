REPORT 01
----------------------------------------------------------------------
New report: Stockout Watch - store SKUs below their order point

I'm a replenishment analyst at Variety Wholesalers (Roses stores). Every morning I want one list of the store and SKU combinations that will run out before the next delivery, worst first, so I can push orders before the shelf goes empty.

Data, all in library PRFDEMOLIB: INVBAL holds one row per SKU (INUMBR) and location (ISTORE) with on hand IBHAND, in transit IBINTQ, transfer on order IBTOOQ, average cost IBAVGC and weekly unit sales IBWK01 to IBWK08. RPLPRF holds the replenishment profile per SKU and store: order-at point RFMIN, order-up-to point RFMAX and vendor lead time RFLEAD in days. INVMST has the item description IDESCR. ITMRTL has the retail price REGPRC for a selling multiple REGMLT, keyed by SKU. TBLSTR has the store city STCITY, state STPVST and supplying warehouse STRWHS.

How it works: only stores (location below 9000) that have a replenishment profile. Average weekly sales is the eight weekly sales columns added up and divided by 8; skip rows that sold nothing in those eight weeks. Available is on hand plus in transit plus transfer on order. Flag the row when available is below the order-at point. Days of cover is on hand divided by average weekly sales times 7. Suggested order is the order-up-to point minus available.

What I'd like to see: store, city, SKU, description, on hand, available, order-at, order-up-to, average weekly sales, days of cover, suggested order units and the retail value of that order. Sort by days of cover, lowest first. Up top: rows flagged, SKUs affected and how many are already at zero.

General: read-only. Trim text fields. Store numbers 9001 and up are distribution centers, not stores. Weekly sales week 01 is the most recent completed week. Severity: red when on hand is zero or less, amber when days of cover is under 7, otherwise yellow. Give every row a plain-English action such as "Order 24 units today".

New report: Out of Stock Today - estimated lost sales

I'm the director of store operations. I want to know which shelves are empty right now on items customers are still buying, and roughly what that costs us every week we leave them empty, so I can lean on the warehouse for the ones that matter.

Data, all in library PRFDEMOLIB: INVBAL has one row per SKU (INUMBR) and location (ISTORE) with on hand IBHAND, in transit IBINTQ and weekly unit sales IBWK01 to IBWK08. ITMRTL has the retail price REGPRC for a selling multiple REGMLT, keyed by SKU, so the unit retail is REGPRC divided by REGMLT. INVMST has the description IDESCR. TBLSTR has the store city STCITY, state STPVST and the warehouse that supplies it, STRWHS. The warehouse's own stock is the INVBAL row for that SKU at the warehouse location number.

How it works: only stores (location below 9000). Average weekly sales is the eight weekly columns added up and divided by 8. A row is out of stock today when on hand is zero or less and the item sold something in the last eight weeks. Estimated weekly lost units is the average weekly sales; estimated weekly lost retail is that times the unit retail. Look up whether the supplying warehouse has stock of the SKU and whether anything is already in transit to the store.

What I'd like to see: store, city, SKU, description, on hand, average weekly sales, estimated weekly lost units and lost retail, units in transit, and the supplying warehouse with its on hand. Sort by lost retail, highest first. Summary at the top: empty shelves, SKUs, total weekly lost units and lost retail, and how many of them the warehouse could fill today.

General: read-only. Trim text fields. Locations 9001 and up are distribution centers. Red when the warehouse has stock but nothing is in transit (a shipping problem we can fix today), amber when the warehouse is also out, green when stock is already in transit. Add a plain-English action on every row.

New report: Chronic Out-of-Stocks - the same shelf empty week after week

I'm the VP of merchandising. One empty week is bad luck; four or more out of eight is a broken replenishment setting or a vendor problem, and customers stop coming back for that item. I want the list of repeat offenders so the buyers fix the root cause.

Data, all in library PRFDEMOLIB: INVBAL has one row per SKU (INUMBR) and location (ISTORE) with out-of-stock flags for the current week IBOSCR and for each of the last eight weeks IBOS01 to IBOS08 (Y means the item was out of stock at some point that week), weekly unit sales IBWK01 to IBWK08, on hand IBHAND and the date it last went out of stock IBALD5 (YYMMDD with century code IBCEN5, 1 = 2000s). INVMST has the description IDESCR, the vendor ASNUM and the department, sub-department and class IDEPT, ISDEPT, ICLAS. APSUPP has the vendor name ASNAME by ASNUM. INVDPT has the class name DPTNAM for the matching IDEPT, ISDEPT and ICLAS where ISCLAS is 0. TBLSTR has the store city STCITY.

How it works: only stores (location below 9000). Count the Y flags across IBOS01 to IBOS08. Keep rows with 4 or more out-of-stock weeks. Estimate lost units as the average sales of the in-stock weeks multiplied by the number of out-of-stock weeks.

What I'd like to see: a summary by vendor and by class (rows, SKUs, average out-of-stock weeks), then the detail with store, SKU, description, class, vendor, weeks out of stock, a little 8-week pattern such as "YYNYYNYY", on hand now and estimated lost units. Sort by weeks out of stock, then lost units.

General: read-only. Trim text fields. Locations 9001 and up are distribution centers. Week 01 is the most recent week. Red for 6 or more weeks out, amber for 4 or 5. Plain-English action per row, for example "Raise the order-at point" or "Talk to the vendor about fill rate".

New report: DC Allocation Shortfall - promises the warehouse cannot keep

I run allocation for our distribution centers. Stores have open transfer requests against each DC, and I need to know where the requests add up to more than the DC actually has on the shelf, before the pickers find out the hard way.

Data, all in library PRFDEMOLIB: TRFDTL is the transfer detail, one line per request, with the shipping location TRFFLC, the receiving store TRFTLC, the SKU INUMBR, quantity requested TRFREQ, quantity shipped TRFSHP, status TRFSTS and batch TRFBCH. INVBAL has on hand IBHAND for every SKU (INUMBR) and location (ISTORE), including the DCs. INVMST has the description IDESCR and vendor ASNUM; APSUPP has the vendor name ASNAME. TBLSTR names the locations by STRNUM with STRNAM.

How it works: every transfer line in the file is still open (nothing has shipped yet). Add up the requested quantity by shipping location and SKU and count the distinct receiving stores. Compare it with the shipping location's on hand from INVBAL for that SKU (no INVBAL row means zero). Shortfall is requested minus on hand when that is positive. Coverage percent is on hand divided by requested.

What I'd like to see: summary by DC (SKUs with open requests, SKUs short, total units short), then the detail: DC name, SKU, description, vendor, open lines, stores waiting, units requested, DC on hand, shortfall and coverage percent. Sort by shortfall, largest first. A chart of shortfall units by DC would be great.

General: read-only. Trim text fields. Receiving store numbers may not all be in TBLSTR; show the number when there is no name. Red when the DC has nothing at all, amber when coverage is under 50 percent, yellow otherwise. Plain-English action per row, such as "Short 576 units: expedite the vendor PO or cut allocations to 19 stores".

New report: Store-to-Store Rebalancing - move stock instead of buying more

I'm an inventory planner. Some stores are about to run out of an item while another store is sitting on months of the same item. Before we buy more, I want to see where a store-to-store transfer would fix both problems.

Data, all in library PRFDEMOLIB: INVBAL has one row per SKU (INUMBR) and location (ISTORE) with on hand IBHAND, in transit IBINTQ, transfer on order IBTOOQ and weekly unit sales IBWK01 to IBWK08. RPLPRF has the order-at point RFMIN and order-up-to point RFMAX per SKU and store. INVMST has the description IDESCR. TBLSTR has the city STCITY, state STPVST and region REGNUM of each store. ITMRTL has the retail REGPRC and multiple REGMLT by SKU.

How it works: only stores (location below 9000). Average weekly sales is the eight weekly columns divided by 8. A store needs stock when it sold something in eight weeks and on hand plus in transit plus transfer on order is below its order-at point; it needs enough to reach the order-up-to point. A store can give when it has more than 26 weeks of supply (spare units are on hand minus 13 weeks of sales), or when it sold nothing in eight weeks and has 6 or more on hand (all of it is spare). For each needing store and SKU, pick the giving store with the most spare units. Suggested move is the smaller of the need and the spare.

What I'd like to see: SKU, description, receiving store and city, its need, giving store and city, its spare units and weeks of supply, suggested units to move and their retail value, and whether both stores are in the same region. Sort by retail value moved, highest first. Summary: moves suggested, units and retail.

General: read-only. Trim text fields. Locations 9001 and up are distribution centers. Green for moves within the same region, amber across regions. Plain-English action per row, such as "Transfer 12 units from Glen Burnie (535) to Laurel (205)".

New report: Trending Items - risers and fallers

I'm a buyer. I want an early warning on items whose chain sales have suddenly jumped, before they sell out at the DC, and on items that have suddenly stalled, before we reorder them.

Data, all in library PRFDEMOLIB: INVCBL is the chain-level balance, one row per SKU (INUMBR), with weekly unit sales CBWK01 to CBWK08 (01 is the most recent week), chain on hand CBHAND, PO on order CBPOOQ and moving annual units CBMASU. INVBAL holds on hand IBHAND by SKU and location (ISTORE); the distribution centers are locations 9001 and up. INVMST has the description IDESCR, vendor ASNUM and buyer BYRNUM. APSUPP has the vendor name ASNAME and plan lead time ASLEAD in days. TBLBYR has the buyer name BYRNAM.

How it works: recent sales are weeks 01 to 04 added up; prior sales are weeks 05 to 08 added up. A riser has recent sales more than 1.5 times prior sales and at least 20 recent units. A faller has recent sales under half of prior sales, with at least 20 prior units. For risers, add DC on hand across the distribution centers and work out DC weeks of supply as DC on hand divided by the recent weekly rate (recent sales divided by 4).

What I'd like to see: two sections. Risers: SKU, description, vendor, buyer, prior 4 weeks, recent 4 weeks, percent change, DC on hand, DC weeks of supply and on order, sorted by percent change. Fallers: SKU, description, prior, recent, percent change, chain on hand and weeks of supply at the new rate. Include a small 8-week sales sparkline per SKU if possible, oldest week on the left.

General: read-only. Trim text fields. For risers, red when DC weeks of supply is under the vendor lead time in weeks, amber under 8 weeks. For fallers, amber. A brand-new item with no prior sales shows as "new". Plain-English action per row, such as "Rising 1,532 percent: reorder now" or "Stalled: hold the next order".

New report: Replenishment Forecast Accuracy

I manage the replenishment system. Store orders are driven by the forecast in each item's replenishment profile, and when the forecast is wrong we either overstock or run out. I want to see where the profile forecast and actual sales have drifted apart, so the team can re-tune those profiles.

Data, all in library PRFDEMOLIB: RPLPRF is one row per SKU (INUMBR) and store (ISTORE) with the forecast average weekly sales RFASWF, base sales RFBSLS, order-at point RFMIN, order-up-to point RFMAX, profile name RFPROF and vendor lead time RFLEAD. INVBAL has, for the same SKU and store, weekly unit sales IBWK01 to IBWK08 and on hand IBHAND. INVMST has the description IDESCR and class codes IDEPT, ISDEPT, ICLAS. INVDPT gives the class name DPTNAM for the matching codes with ISCLAS 0.

How it works: actual weekly sales is the eight weekly columns divided by 8. Only profile rows that sold at least 8 units in those eight weeks. Forecast ratio is forecast divided by actual. Over-forecast when the ratio is above 1.5, under-forecast when it is below 0.67. The error in units per week is forecast minus actual.

What I'd like to see: a summary (profiles checked, over, under, within range, and the average ratio), a breakdown by class with how many profiles are over and under, and the detail: store, SKU, description, profile, forecast, actual, ratio, order-at, order-up-to and on hand. Sort by the size of the error, largest first. A scatter chart of forecast against actual would make the point nicely.

General: read-only. Trim text fields. Red for under-forecast (stockout risk), amber for over-forecast (overstock risk), green within range. Plain-English action per row, such as "Forecast 3 a week, selling 9: raise the forecast".

New report: GMROI and Turns by Class

I'm the CFO. I want to know which classes earn their keep: gross margin dollars returned for every dollar we hold in inventory, and how fast the stock turns. The ones at the bottom should get less space and less open-to-buy.

Data, all in library PRFDEMOLIB: INVBAL has one row per SKU (INUMBR) and location (ISTORE) with year-to-date sales dollars IBRSDY, year-to-date cost of those sales IBRCSY, on hand IBHAND, average cost IBAVGC, and the system's own GMROI IBGMRI and turns IBTRNS for that row. INVMST has IDEPT, ISDEPT, ICLAS and the description IDESCR. INVDPT gives the sub-department name (ICLAS 0 and ISCLAS 0) and class name (ISCLAS 0) in DPTNAM.

How it works: only stores (location below 9000). Gross margin dollars are sales dollars minus cost dollars. Inventory at cost is on hand times average cost. For a class, take the GMROI and turns fields weighted by sales dollars (sum of field times sales, divided by total sales). Margin percent is margin dollars divided by sales dollars. Share of sales is the class's sales divided by all sales.

What I'd like to see: a ranked class table (sub-department, class, SKUs, sales, margin dollars, margin percent, inventory at cost, GMROI, turns, share of sales and share of inventory), sorted by GMROI, lowest first. A quadrant chart of GMROI against turns, or a bar chart of GMROI by class. Underneath, the ten SKUs with the most inventory dollars and a GMROI under 2.

General: read-only. Trim text fields. Locations 9001 and up are distribution centers. Red for GMROI under 2, amber 2 to 5, green above 5. Plain-English action per class, such as "Earns 16 dollars per inventory dollar: protect the space" or "Earns nothing: cut the assortment".
